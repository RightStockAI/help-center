# Traditional Analysis

Traditional Analysis is the finance-first stock report in RightStockAI. Use it when you want to review business quality, valuation, technical context, risk, and analyst-style summary before comparing AI views.

[Start Traditional Analysis](https://www.rightstockai.com/analysis/single/traditional)

## When to Use It

Use Traditional Analysis when you want to answer:

- Is the company financially healthy?
- Does valuation look stretched, fair, or attractive?
- What do common technical indicators suggest?
- What risks does the report highlight?
- How does the finance-first view compare with AI-assisted views?

## What the Report May Include

### Summary and Score

The summary gives a quick view of the stock. The score helps you compare stocks, but it should not be read alone.

Always read the explanation behind the score.

### Recommendation Context

Traditional Analysis may present a recommendation-style label or score. Read it as a compact summary of the report, not as a personal instruction.

A useful way to read the recommendation:

- **Positive view:** check whether valuation, trend, and financial health support the view.
- **Neutral view:** look for mixed signals or unclear risk/reward.
- **Cautious view:** read which weakness is driving the caution before dismissing the stock.

### Fundamentals

Fundamentals describe the company behind the stock. This can include revenue, profit, margins, debt, cash flow, valuation ratios, and other available financial context.

Plain-language reading:

- Strong fundamentals can support a longer-term thesis.
- Weak fundamentals can make a stock riskier even when the chart looks strong.
- Valuation matters because a good company can still be expensive.

Common fundamental terms:

| Term | Simple meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Revenue | Money the company earns from operations. |
| Profit | What remains after costs and expenses. |
| Margin | How much profit the company keeps from sales. |
| Debt | Borrowed money the company must manage. |
| Cash flow | Cash moving in and out of the business. |
| P/E ratio | Price compared with earnings. Useful for valuation context. |
| P/B ratio | Price compared with book value. Often used for asset-heavy businesses. |
| ROE | How efficiently the company uses shareholder money. |
| Debt-to-equity | How much debt the company uses compared with shareholder capital. |

### Technical Context

Technical context studies price and volume behavior. It can include trend, momentum, support, resistance, and indicator-style signals.

Use it to understand timing and structure, not to predict with certainty.

Common technical terms:

| Term | Simple meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Trend | The broad direction of price movement. |
| Support | A price area where buyers may appear. |
| Resistance | A price area where sellers may appear. |
| Moving average | A smoothed price line used to understand trend. |
| RSI | A momentum reading that can show whether a move is stretched. |
| MACD | A trend and momentum indicator used to spot shifts. |
| Volume | How many shares traded. Volume helps judge interest behind a move. |

### Risk Notes

Risk notes may cover volatility, weak financials, valuation pressure, trend weakness, concentration risk, or other caution points.

Read risks before acting on a positive summary.

Risk terms you may see:

| Term | Simple meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Volatility | How sharply a stock moves up and down. |
| Drawdown | How far a stock or portfolio falls from a previous high. |
| Beta | How sensitive a stock is compared with the broader market. |
| Concentration | Too much exposure to one stock, sector, or theme. |
| Liquidity | How easy it may be to buy or sell without large price impact. |

## How to Read Traditional Analysis

1. Confirm the stock name and symbol.
2. Read the overall summary.
3. Check the score and what drove it.
4. Review fundamentals.
5. Review technical context.
6. Read risk notes and caution points.
7. Compare with Advanced AI or Smart AI Analysis.

## Good Follow-Up Questions

After reading the report, ask:

- Does the summary match the risk notes?
- Is the stock strong because of business quality, price momentum, or both?
- Is the valuation reasonable for the growth and risk?
- Is this stock already in my portfolio or only on a watchlist?
- Does another analysis view agree or disagree?

## Use Cases

### Long-Term Research

Use Traditional Analysis when you care about business quality, valuation, debt, profitability, and the durability of the company.

### Shortlist Review

Use it to compare multiple stocks from the same sector. A stock that looks strong alone may look less attractive beside a better peer.

### Risk Review

Use it when a stock has already moved sharply and you want to know whether the move is supported by fundamentals or mainly by momentum.

### Portfolio Check

Use it for important holdings in your portfolio. A stock-level report can explain what is helping or hurting the portfolio view.

## How to Compare Two Stocks

When comparing two companies, avoid comparing only the final score.

Check:

1. Business quality.
2. Valuation.
3. Profitability.
4. Debt and cash flow.
5. Trend and momentum.
6. Risk notes.
7. Report age.

If one stock has a higher score but much higher risk, the lower-scoring stock may still be more suitable for some users.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating a score as the whole answer.
- Ignoring valuation when momentum is strong.
- Ignoring risk notes when the recommendation looks positive.
- Comparing reports without checking when each report was generated.
- Using a company-level report without considering your portfolio exposure.
